All you ever needed to know about buses in rural Mexico:

1. Enjoy the views. Some of them are spectacular.
2. Know how to say the name of your destination. Ixcaquixtla, Molcaxac and Huajuapan are not intuitive names for English speakers.
3. Ask a lot of questions. Bus stops are not marked and routes change but in general people were pretty friendly. At one stop, they insisted the piece of unmarked curb we were sitting on was indeed a bus stop and 2.5 hours later, they were right.
4. Don't be in a hurry. Our 134 km "morning" trip took over 7 hours.

Another bus ride through beautiful mountains with fog filled valleys. On a mission to find the town of Molcaxac. We can see it on Google maps ... now just need to find the combination of buses to get there.

The buses were definitely not first class (turns out bus drivers in Mexico are also mechanics)

We were given several different alternate routes, all involving at least 3 buses and in some also including a taxi ride in the middle. The "bus stops" were all things like "the intersection with the road " Acatlán-Ixcaquixtla. Get out there."  or "in the back of the main church".

With several broken windows, a door that doesn't close and a horrible starting sound, one can understand why they added a crucifix. Several hours to go. Thanks to all the friendly people that directed us the right way!

Either we are excessively health conscious or they get sick a lot. Raw chicken.

Buses have layovers too. Sometimes 2.5 hour layovers with no where to sit.

Bus driver is texting and driving. Not just a little bit. He's driving down the highway looking at his phone.

A first class bus! It has a complete set of windows but standing room only for those that didn't pay for a seat. Visited Molcaxac, met city officials and now headed to Puebla for the night.

When you pay a quarter to use the bathroom, they give you a very nice receipt.
